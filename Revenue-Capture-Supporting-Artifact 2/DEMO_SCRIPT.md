# Revenue Capture — video demo script

Recommended raw recording: **2 minutes 30 seconds**. At **1.25× speed**, this becomes approximately two minutes. Read only the “Say” text. The clicks and silent scrolls are recording directions.

This sequence follows: Connections → add a WhatsApp policy → Overview → Nila → Open investigation → review and draft → settlement destination → Value realisation → Settings.

## Prepare before recording

- English interface, desktop viewport, expanded sidebar, agents running.
- Start on Connections with Nila Foods unreviewed. Reset before recording if the case has already been paid, dismissed or a source rejected.
- Keep a small caption: **“Illustrative walkthrough · simulated connections, AI interpretation and payments.”**
- Rehearse the complete route once. Saving a new connection saves a configuration; it does not authorise or scan a real account.
- Copy the additional-instructions text below so you can paste it instead of slowly typing during the recording.

## Main recording: narration and clicks

### 1. Opening and Connections — 0:00–0:25

**Do:** Start at the top of Connections. Scroll steadily through accounting, delivery, business email, WhatsApp and CRM cards. Continue down to coverage/data boundaries and processing limits. Show those controls without explaining every field. Return to the top, click **Add connection**, and pause briefly over the channel choices.

**Say:**

“Revenue Capture helps merchants find approved, completed work missing from invoices. It brings the evidence together before the merchant decides to bill.”

“Connections brings accounting, delivery, email, WhatsApp and CRM sources into one workflow. Merchants choose the access boundaries before reviewing any charge.”

**Silent visual:** Coverage and processing limits. Let the viewer see them without reading out the settings.

### 2. Add a WhatsApp policy — 0:25–0:55

**Do:** Click **WhatsApp Business**. Show the provider, account label, conversation selection, history, daily limit and retention. Use the suggested entries above. Click **Continue**.

Briefly show **Meaning / intent → Approved extras**. Switch **Match by** to **Exact keywords**, enter `approved, confirmed`, show the exclusions and paste the additional instructions. Click **Preview policy**. Scroll through the included/excluded records and reasons. Tick **I reviewed this processing policy**, then click **Save connection**.

**Say:**

“Here I define a WhatsApp processing policy: which conversations, how much history, and record limits. I can select meaning or keywords, exclusions and additional instructions. The preview explains what is included or excluded. Saving stores the policy; live authorisation is separate.”

**Show, without extra narration:** The saved connection's authorisation-pending state.

### 3. Overview — 0:55–1:07

**Do:** Click **Overview**. Scroll once from the three metrics through the suggested next step and opportunity list. Click the **Nila Foods · NS-1072** case directly. If it is not first, use its name rather than opening a different case.

**Say:**

“Overview separates supported unbilled work, missing evidence and confirmed collections. Each opportunity opens into the records and calculation behind it. Let’s review Nila Foods.”

### 4. Open investigation: show changing evidence — 1:07–1:43

**Do:** On the Nila page, scroll to **Open investigation** and click it. Show the initial finding. Click **Find completion & later messages** and pause over the revised ₹2,000 candidate. Click **Add conflicting customer message** to show the billing hold. Then click **Inspect simulated clarification**. Close the dialog using the top-right **×**.

**Say:**

“Nila’s approval and completion use different wording. Open investigation shows a separate example of how the proposed AI would review changing evidence. Later messages reduce the quantity to 100, giving ₹2,000. A package dispute then blocks billing; clarification restores a candidate for review. This sequence is scripted and does not change Nila’s ₹2,400 charge.”

**Why this is the central AI moment:** The viewer sees scope revision, conflicting evidence and a hold, rather than only an AI label or a successful match.

### 5. Merchant verification and invoice draft — 1:43–2:03

**Do:** Back on Nila, click **Jump to decision ↓**. Confirm the three review checks for separately approved scope/pricing, completion and absence of prior billing. Click **Prepare invoice draft**. Show the recipient, description and ₹2,400 amount. Point to editing controls; leave the draft for review.

**Say:**

“Back on Nila, I verify the agreed scope and price, completion and no prior billing. I can edit, ignore or reject the charge. Only after these checks do I prepare an invoice draft. Sending still requires explicit approval.”

**Keep this take focused:** You do not need to send, accept or match a payment. Draft preparation alone must not increase collections.

### 6. Where the money arrives — 2:03–2:08

**Do:** Scroll to the settlement/bank destination panel below the decision area. Show the masked account and settlement information for a few seconds.

**Say:** Nothing. This is a silent visual transition.

Do not claim that preparing the draft sends money to the account.

### 7. Value realisation and filters — 2:08–2:20

**Do:** Click **Value realisation**. Select **Last 30 days**, then click **Apply**. Scroll through collected cash, pending value, review/hold counts and the visualisation. Use **All customers** and **All stages** so the view is not accidentally restricted.

**Say:**

“Value realisation tracks pending opportunities, review bottlenecks and collected cash. Date and stage filters help focus the view. Collections increase only when a payment is matched—not when I prepare a draft.”

**Optional alternative:** Use **Custom date range** instead of Last 30 days, with a range including the displayed detection dates. For the October walkthrough, 1–31 October 2026 covers the cases. Date filters select the opportunity-detection cohort, not just payments received within the period. Avoid spending time typing a range during the take unless rehearsed.

### 8. Account and Settings — 2:20–2:30

**Do:** Click **Settings**. Use the jump links to briefly show **Bank & settlements**, **Business profile**, **Appearance & language**, **Accessibility**, and **Data & privacy**. Show the controls without editing accounts or deleting data.

**Say:**

“Account and Settings brings together settlement details, business preferences, language, accessibility and data controls. The merchant stays in control from source access through billing.”

## Optional closing shots

If you have another five to ten seconds, show **Activity & Approvals**, then **Help & support** and **Ask Ray**. You can use these as silent closing shots or say:

“Activity records the decisions; Help and Ask Ray provide guidance.”

Ask Ray is the current UI name. Its responses are scripted guidance. Keep these shots shorter than the evidence investigation.

## Editing priorities

1. Preserve the opening, connection policy preview, evidence conflict/hold and merchant verification.
2. Speed up silent scrolling, pasting and navigation first. Avoid speeding through the figures in the investigation.
3. Keep the distinction between the separate ₹2,000 investigation and Nila’s ₹2,400 draft audible.
4. Shorten Settings or optional support shots before cutting the main evidence-to-invoice story.
5. Do not describe listed providers as live integrations, saved instructions as executed AI, a draft as recovered cash, or accessibility controls as certified compliance.

The strongest closing impression is a merchant-controlled workflow: permissioned evidence → reviewable proposal → verified draft → separately tracked collection.
